Website Redesign

Restructuring a website that helps students and families plan how to pay for college, so people can find and understand what they need. A project for my Information Architecture course at UT Austin.

01 · Challenge

Useful content, hard to navigate and find

The site has a lot of useful content about paying for college, but there's so much of it, spread across overlapping menus, that people struggle to find what they need. Pages are also visually cluttered, so it's hard to tell what matters most at a glance.

Goal

Understand how the content is organized today and how users expect it to be organized, so we could design a structure that helps them find and understand it.

02 · Research

First the site, then the people using it

The whole site was crawled with Screaming Frog, and a content inventory and audit was performed. Every page was logged in a spreadsheet to capture what's on the site, and how it's labeled and organized. We then reviewed each page in depth, flagging empty, broken and duplicate pages.

47empty pages
19broken links
46duplicate or near-duplicate pages

Completing the inventory gave us a full look at what we were working with and informed our card sort exercise, aimed at seeing how people naturally group this content. In think-aloud sessions, 11 participants sorted the cards into groups of their own while explaining their choices. Interview notes and findings were mapped out in an affinity diagram.

03 · Insights

Built on internal logic, mismatched mental models

Three patterns came out of the audit and card sort, and each one points to a change in the redesign.

LanguagePeople don't speak the site's language. Participants stumbled on terms like "PLUS loan."→ Use plain, consistent labels that first-time visitors understand.
StructurePeople sort by topic, but the site's menus mix stage, audience and format (see the navigation below), and content isn't tagged consistently.→ Build the navigation around users' existing mental model, and tag content consistently.
FindabilityImportant content is buried or broken. 7 of 11 participants expected "Scholarships" next to "Application Deadlines," not under "Paying." The audit also turned up empty, broken and duplicate pages, and visually cluttered pages where key information doesn't stand out.→ Clean up the content, place it where people look for it, and simplify cluttered pages.
04 · Next steps

Rebuilding the structure around how users think

We presented these findings to the client. Our guiding question now: how might we organize the site so students and families can find and understand what they need to plan how to pay for college?

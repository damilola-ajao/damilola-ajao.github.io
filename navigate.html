NaviGATE

A mobile game that helps people learn routes by heart, so they lean less on GPS. A project for my Human-Computer Interaction course at UT Austin.

My roleUX researcher & designer
Research, wireframes, prototyping, testing
Team4
TimelineFall 2025
ToolsFigma, Google Forms
Contents
  1. Problem
  2. Solution
  3. What I learned
01 · Problem

People follow GPS, but never learn the route

To understand why, we surveyed people about how they get around and what they enjoy in mobile games, interviewed ten more, and grouped the interview notes into an affinity diagram.

24survey responses
10interviews
117work activity notes
  • 91.6% use GPS almost always or all the time.
  • 75% were neutral to uncomfortable describing a familiar route without it.
Key insight

Almost everyone relied on GPS, and few could describe a route they take every week without it.

We designed for Alice, a persona who relies on GPS, plays in short bursts, and gets discouraged when she fails early.

02 · Solution

A game that looks like the map you already use

Alice already knows Google and Apple Maps, so the game borrows that layout. Then it takes the help away in three levels: Level 0 gives every turn, Level 1 gives only hints and a health bar for wrong turns, and Level 2 combines learned routes into one trip. Players unlock each level as they go, the game feature people liked most in our survey.

03 · What I learned

What testing showed, and what I'd do next

We ran a think-aloud pilot test on the Figma prototype, then another team evaluated it against Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics.

Pilot"Level 0" didn't say what to doRenamed it "Play route."
PilotRoute lists crowded the screenGave the lists more space.
HeuristicThe health bar looked like a progress barRecommended hearts instead.

Next time

Our tester said it felt more like a maps app than a game, and I agree. Next, I'd push the visual identity further with story and characters, move sign-up to the start so levels can be tailored from day one, and test with people over 35, since everyone we spoke to was 18 to 35.
